Date: 2026-10-09

## Direction and scope

Reading this as: a personal technical writing site for readers interested in Carlos's projects, with restrained editorial structure and an existing terminal/ASCII identity. Observed dials: ENERGY 1 / RHYTHM 2 / MOTION 1 on posts and articles. The reference contributes hierarchy, space, useful navigation, and content-specific figures. Its wordmark, blue palette, and custom illustrations are not site assets to copy. The site's documented neutral palette, theme toggle, centered home profile, and ASCII homepage motif remain the direction.

I compared the reference's chapters index and “How does a screen work?” article with the local production preview of `/posts/` and `/posts/nodestrikeexecution/` in Chrome. I checked the post in dark and light modes and scrolled into its diagrams. I inspected the Hugo templates and post front matter. The production preview built successfully with Hugo 0.147.7. No site code or content was changed in this audit.

## Numbered findings

1. **HIGH: Theme switching breaks article diagrams (R-34, theme resilience).** Three Mermaid diagrams initially render on the NodeStrike article. After clicking the theme toggle, they become large “Syntax error in text mermaid version 10.9.8” blocks in the light theme. `layouts/partials/footer.html` attempts to rerun Mermaid after stripping `data-processed`, but rendered diagrams do not retain the original source in `data-mermaid-src`. This is the most urgent post UI fix because real explanatory content disappears.

2. **LOW: The posts index includes non-articles (R-05, content-driven composition).** `/posts/` includes “terms” and “About Me” among project articles. The terms entry exposes raw cookie-policy HTML in its preview. Readers looking for posts must scan past unrelated material. This repeats finding 6 from audit 001.

3. **LOW: Automatic excerpts make the posts list hard to scan (R-05, content-driven composition).** PaperMod's `themes/hugo-PaperMod/layouts/_default/list.html` uses `.Summary` for every entry. The NodeStrike preview begins with a Plotly attribution, then runs into a section heading; Cube Kill exposes button text; the terms preview starts with `<!DOCTYPE html>`. The excerpt length follows the automatic cutoff, not the article's point. Add short, authored descriptions to published articles and let the list show those. Do not invent article claims.

4. **LOW: Internal task notes appear at the end of a published article (R-05, content-driven composition).** `/posts/nodestrikeexecution/` ends with a `TODO` heading, unchecked tasks, and internal reminders from `content/Posts/nodeStrikeExecution.md`. That is not a reader-facing ending. This repeats finding 7 from audit 001.

## Reference-informed UI direction

These are design choices, not rule violations. They require the owner's selection under After mode.

- **Editorial post list:** use flat rows with fine separators, an intentional description, and quieter date/reading-time metadata. Make each article title the focal point. Retain real, descriptive titles; avoid decorative numbering that implies a series the site does not have.
- **Article opening:** give the title and an authored one-sentence deck more room, then place date and reading time on a separate quiet line. Move “Suggest Changes” out of the metadata sentence. Preserve the existing light/dark toggle and neutral palette.
- **Long-form navigation:** make the existing table of contents easier to find on wide screens, while keeping it compact on phones. A sticky side rail is a larger change and should be tested with long headings and the existing charts.
- **Figures and small details:** give existing diagrams and benchmark charts consistent spacing and meaningful captions tied to the actual data. Use monospace only for dates, labels, code, or figure markers where it helps scanning. Do not manufacture illustrations or chart labels.
